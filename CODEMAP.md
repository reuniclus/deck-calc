| `src/math/mulligan.ts` | EXACT optimal mulligan strategy: enumerate every possible hand composition, recursively compare keep vs. mulligan-and-retry (a fresh full-size hand from the smaller remaining deck), weighted by each hand's own multivariate-hypergeometric probability. No simulation anywhere. Deliberately NOT real London rules (kept hand always full-size, not shrinking) -- an explicit, agreed simplification. Only models the OPENING HAND point exactly; turns 1+ still draw from a single continuous shuffle as before. A real, confirmed consequence: bestP comes out HIGHER than the naive independent-attempts formula 1-(1-p)^(mulligans+1), because a failing hand enriches what's left in the deck -- verified numerically, not assumed. `optimalMulliganCurve` generalizes this to a WHOLE curve (every draw count at once, not just one goal turn) -- evaluate() computes the whole curve internally regardless, the scalar version was simply discarding everything except one index; cross-validated exactly against the scalar function at multiple points. MEMOIZED by `(remaining group counts, mulligans left)`: the recursive value at any point depends only on that state, not the specific sequence of hands that reached it, and different hand sequences routinely land on the same cumulative state (removal is addition, addition is commutative). Measured directly: 2 groups/1 mulligan went from ~500ms to ~99ms, 2 groups/2 mulligans from several seconds to ~164ms, and 3-group cases that were previously rejected outright (too large under the pre-memoization cap) now complete in 1-3s. The size cap (`checkSizeCap`) was recalibrated to reflect the actual memoized cost (sum of per-level state counts) instead of the pre-memoization total PATH count (a product, which had been a severe overestimate) -- and raised further since the computation now runs through a Worker anyway, so a multi-second case no longer needs to be avoided at all costs, just shown with a loading state |

| `src/math/reveal.ts` | the ONE "condition on a reveal, project forward" primitive both mulligan.ts and the selection-effect model are special cases of (PLAN.md's unification section). A reveal is three separate facts kept deliberately apart: what left the unseen pool (`comp`/`total` -- hand/bottomed/exiled/milled are one fact here), what counts toward the query (`secured`, a subset), and how many cards get drawn afterward (not part of the reveal, hence a returned curve). `shiftBox`/`shiftDnf`/`poolAfter`/`enumerateReveals` live here now; mulligan.ts calls them instead of owning copies. Refactor verified byte-identical, not just green |
| `src/math/selection.ts` | card-selection effects (draw/scry/impulse/ponder) as ORDINARY tracked groups -- no dilution machinery (it belongs only to the exploratory Questions tool, see PLAN.md correction 2). Settled taxonomy as four mechanical axes (examined, keepMax, keptCostsDraw, nonKeptLeavesPool) rather than a case per card name. `exactDrawCurve` is a sequential SLOT DP: it tracks (consumed, copies consumed, scheduled slots used, window credits) and NO group dimensions, because the query's composition is conditionally hypergeometric given the slot structure -- one `evaluate()` on the pool minus every copy supplies it. "No cascading" IS the credits>0 branch, not an assumption on top. Matches bruteSelection.ts to ~1e-15 over 32 cases; both previously plausible closed forms were measured wrong by up to 8 points with a sign that flips by effect type, and deleted. `slotDistribution` is split out and cached because it's query-independent (117ms cold / 11.5ms warm at 99 cards). All four shapes now implemented for a SINGLE-group threshold query and verified against bruteSelection.ts to 2.2e-16: `exactSelectionCurveSingleGroup` resolves windows ATOMICALLY (needed for ponder, whose shuffle-or-keep decision sees the whole window; also removes the credits dimension), while `exactDrawCurve`/`exactScryCurveSingleGroup` are independent derivations of the same numbers kept as cross-checks. Scry carries group state because the PLAY depends on card identity; it stays small because success absorbs, needed cards are never bottomed, and running out of draws absorbs. Impulse tracks CONSUMED separately from ACQUIRED -- an impulse capped at one keep exiles the second useful card, and deriving remaining counts from acquired overstated it by 2 points. Ponder's reorder branch has provably zero net advance (the two halves cancel), so its whole value is the shuffle option. `exactSelectionCurveAnd` covers an AND of thresholds over several groups, with the keep decision as a genuine max over commit vectors ("take the one you lack" is an output, not a rule). Canonicalizes satisfied groups into the filler pool (exact; biggest speedup) and packs state into numeric mixed-radix keys -- 7.5s to 1.6s for a 60-card two-group case, though that's still worker-only and NOT cacheable across grid rows the way slotDistribution is, since this DP does depend on the query. Upper bounds ARE supported via `TrackedGroup.hi` (0 = brick/garnet you must not draw): success stops absorbing, bounded groups can't be folded, and declining a card becomes a real move -- which inverts the effect ranking, since drawing forces cards into hand and can be WORSE than having no effect while looking refuses. `hi=0` is cheaper than monotone (absorbing failure prunes, 627ms); "at most k" is the expensive shape (1762ms). Bounded curves DECREASE in draws, so monotone-only consumers (frontier/advisor) must stay dark for them. OR across clauses is still unimplemented -- mechanical plus state growth, not a missing concept -- and the signature takes groups rather than a Dnf so it can't be passed one by accident |
| `src/math/modifiedQuery.ts` | fast UPPER BOUND for capped-keep effects (impulse/surveil), for where the exact DP is too slow. Built on `hold = seen - ditched`: whatever you had to let go shifts the query, lower bounds and brick caps alike, so bricks need no special case. A relaxation (keep budget pooled across windows; keeps chosen with all windows visible), so it dominates the exact value -- every measured deviation is positive. Tightness depends on the draw HORIZON as much as the deck: +0.02pt at 15 draws, +0.20pt at 10 on the same query, +0.76pt on a 12-card deck. Worst where the DP is cheapest, which is what makes "DP when affordable, this when not" safe. NOT valid for scry-shaped effects (see PLAN.md: +2.5pt from a timing leak) |
| `src/math/modifiedQueryScry.ts` | **research module, NOT shipping** -- the scry variant of the modified-query method, promoted to a real file after being rebuilt from scratch inside throwaway test files six times in one session (which made cross-variant comparisons unreliable). Worst residual still ~1.4pt, diagnosed as a mean-field bias from iterating on E[keeps] rather than its distribution. `ScryInversionError` guards the regime where the trigger inversion is invalid (large look sizes truncate windows and saturate `seen`) rather than answering wrongly. `exactSelectionCurveDnf` remains the only shipping path for scry |
| `src/math/validationReport.ts` | **test-only** standard format for validity/sanity checks: `config | reference=value | cand=value | signed d in pt | mass | verdict | times`, with a named reference-strength ladder (analytic > brute > clairvoyant-bound > exact-dp > degenerate) and a fixed verdict vocabulary. | `src/math/forwardScry.ts` | **test-only** independent exact cross-check: pushes probability MASS forward from the opening hand rather than computing a backward value function, so it shares no code with the DP it validates. Agrees to 1e-12 at 60 cards, where `bruteSelection.ts` caps out near 12 -- the only exact check available at realistic deck sizes. `epsilon` prunes low-mass states and banks what was dropped, giving a rigorous interval `[p, p+dropped]` (0.0002-0.0045pt wide at 1e-9). NOT a faster route than the DP and nothing ships on it: pruning removes only ~a third of states because the mass is not concentrated, and interval width is roughly (pruned states x epsilon) so it cannot be pushed. Scry-shaped, monotone AND queries only -- forward propagation cannot max over decisions, so OR and upper bounds would give a lower bound rather than an answer |
| `src/math/bruteSelection.ts` | **test-only** independent arbiter: every distinct ordering of a small labelled deck, played out with real mechanics (top of library, bottoming, exiling, hand).
